**Tuesday, August 25, 5 PM, Council Chamber, second floor of City Hall, 70 Court Plaza.** The Flock resolution is New Business item VI.A ([agenda](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1KQx06L9DUN1ReoOSCPcS_fwl8cJ1YlypJJjihwGwFWc/edit) · [resolution](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1uhGDUZtpecu4I31Y2ePjYNk1AcM4BPXVP7TbSrRbs40/edit)). Speakers sign up at the chamber door; be there by 4:30. Emailed and voicemail comments go into the record only if received **by 9:00 a.m. Tuesday** ([the city's rules](https://publicinput.com/g1267)).

## The short version

Council is about to end the Flock contract. Good: residents forced that. But council cannot rewrite a contract from the dais, so the useful asks on Tuesday are the ones a council member can make as an **amendment to the resolution** during the meeting. Three of them:

1. **End the Flock contract.** Support the resolution.
2. **Amend it so the pause covers every plate reader, not one vendor.** The resolution's Section 6 freezes new *Flock* technology during the review. It should freeze automated license plate readers from any vendor, fixed or vehicle-mounted, including the Axon readers the chief's memo plans to expand.
3. **Amend it so the review is real.** Public hearings, a public comment period, and an independent reviewer the community helps choose, with the report published before council acts on it.

Why one vendor isn't enough:

- **The plan is already to replace Flock with Axon.** Chief Stepp's August 14 memo, attached to Tuesday's agenda, says APD "is not pursuing a full transition away from Flock," will be "expanding and integrating Axon's automated license plate reader capabilities," and expects "the Axon ALPR infrastructure operational" by February 2027 ([memo](https://docs.google.com/document/d/13kwmbwt6T9PiEjUgrFNb9ZHFGzaIsHi5cvsBHkpq-lY/edit) · [[Getting Flock Out|scored against the eight demands]]).
- **The resolution doesn't touch the system that already does most of the reading.** APD says it runs **11 Flock cameras and 162 Axon plate readers in marked patrol cars**, the Axon readers since April 2024 ([828NewsNow, Aug 20](https://828newsnow.com/news/228822-ending-flock-wouldnt-end-ashevilles-license-plate-surveillance/)). Ending Flock removes eleven readers and leaves 162. The city says both systems have public dashboards; the Axon one cannot be found on the city's site.
- **Nobody has seen what the city is buying.** The executed Axon contract, promised for mid-August, has not been released ([[Promise Tracker|Promise Tracker, row 12]]), and a second Axon agreement, for 20 more in-car readers, went to signature in July and was never announced to council ([[The RTIC Policy|the released emails]]).

Council members have said on the record that the constitutional questions apply to the technology, not the logo. The mayor, August 20: replacing Flock with Axon stationary readers means "the question around constitutional protection would come back into play... we need to have a discussion about it for sure" ([[2026-08-20 Agenda Briefing]]). The amendment asks her to put that sentence in the resolution.

## The amendment, written out

A council member can move any of these in the meeting. Hand them the text.

**Section 6, vendor-neutral (the main ask).** Replace Section 6 with:

> During the review described above, the City shall not purchase, lease, activate, deploy, or expand automated license plate reader technology from any vendor, whether fixed or vehicle-mounted, and shall not enter into any new or amended agreement for such technology, without a separate vote of City Council taken at a public meeting after public comment.

The fallback, if a full pause cannot get four votes: keep the sentence above but strike "or expand," so that existing in-car readers continue but nothing new goes up without a public vote. That is the mayor's August 20 statement, made binding.

**Section 2, the network, not just the cameras.** Add after the first sentence:

> The City Manager shall terminate all City user accounts, search licenses, and network lookup access to Flock Safety systems, including access to data collected by other agencies.

Without this, the memo's "Flock software license that would preserve access to the Flock network" is still on the table ([[Getting Flock Out|why the network is the product]]).

**Section 5, a real evaluation.** Add:

> The review shall include at least two public hearings, a public comment period of not less than thirty days on draft recommendations, and an independent civil-liberties reviewer selected through an open process with community participation. The reviewer's report shall be published before City Council takes any action on it.

**Sections 1 and 3, prove the exit.** In Section 1, after "remove their hardware," add "no later than thirty days after adoption, with removal publicly confirmed." In Section 3, add: "The City Manager shall request written certification from Flock Safety that all City data has been deleted and shall publish it."

**A new section, the paper.** Add:

> Within fourteen days, the City Manager shall release the executed Axon agreements, including the July 2026 agreement for vehicle-mounted readers and all pricing schedules, and shall publish the current sharing list and retention settings for every automated license plate reader system the City operates.

## Speak on Tuesday

- **Sign up at the door.** In-person speakers must sign up at the chamber; the list order decides who gets in under the one-hour cap per item. Be there by 4:30.
- **Sign up for item VI.A**, the Flock resolution. You can also sign up for the general public comment period at the end (item VII) to speak on surveillance more broadly; that is a second three minutes.
- **It may be late.** Item VI.A comes after the consent agenda and two public hearings, one of them a citywide change allowing duplexes in every residential district, which will draw its own crowd. Plan to stay; the mayor committed to overflow rooms on the sixth floor.
- **Three minutes each, or ten for a group.** The rules allow "a person representing a group (speaker plus three others) present in the council chamber" ten minutes. Four neighbors who agree on one ask get more time than four separate speakers.
- **Split the asks.** Twenty people making the same point spends twenty slots on one argument. Decide beforehand who takes Section 6, who takes Section 2, who takes the review, who takes the documents, who takes the sheriff's log. The [[Speak at Council|three-minute structure]]: who you are and your neighborhood, one sourced fact, one specific amendment, and that you'll be back.
- **If the clock runs out**, your written comment is still in the record. Send it anyway.

## Email council

Two addresses, two jobs:

- **avl-city-council@publicinput.com** puts your comment in the meeting record. **Deadline: 9:00 a.m. Tuesday.**
- **ashevillenccouncil@ashevillenc.gov** reaches all seven members directly, any time. Send to both.

Keep it under 150 words. Subject line: **Aug 25, item VI.A: amend Section 6 to cover every plate reader.** A template:

> Council members,
>
> I live in [neighborhood]. I support ending the Flock contract on Tuesday, and I'm asking you to amend the resolution before you pass it.
>
> Amend Section 6 so the pause covers automated license plate readers from any vendor, fixed or in-car, not only Flock. Chief Stepp's memo says the plan is to expand Axon readers by February 2027; APD already runs 162 of them. Ending one vendor does not answer the constitutional concerns residents have raised for a year.
>
> Amend Section 5 so the review includes public hearings and an independent reviewer the community helps choose, with the report published before you act on it.
>
> Please say which way you vote on the amendment, not only the resolution.
>
> [Name, neighborhood]

Three things that make it land: your neighborhood in the first line, one section number, and a request for their position on the amendment so silence is an answer. Things that don't: long histories, national statistics without a local hook, and anger at staff.
