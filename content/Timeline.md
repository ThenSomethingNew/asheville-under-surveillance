## The county builds it first (2022–2023)

**June 2022: Buncombe County's 2,000-camera network surfaces.** The Citizen Times reports that Sheriff Quentin Miller's office is running roughly 2,000 cameras on the Fusus platform (about 1,500 inside county schools, another 500 across housing communities and businesses) feeding a real-time intelligence center most residents had never heard of. The Sheriff proposes expanding downtown along the courthouse corridor and Patton Avenue, with businesses paying $60 per camera per year to join. ([Citizen Times via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/2-000-camera-network-feeding-090126108.html))

**January 24, 2023: Asheville plugs in, 4–1.** City Council approves an interlocal agreement giving APD access to the county's Fusus center (roughly 1,800 cameras at the time) for $30,000 a year, plus city cameras at City Hall, Pritchard Park, and North Lexington. **Kim Roney casts the lone no vote**, a position she will hold alone for the next three years. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-police-get-access-to-buncombe-county-sheriffs-office-fusus-real-time-intelligence-center-camera-system-collaboration-emergency-situations), [Mountain Xpress](https://mountainx.com/news/asheville-joins-buncombe-county-surveillance-system/))

## The quiet buildout (2024–2025)

**2024: The money is requested before the public knows.** In April 2024, APD and the office of U.S. Rep. [[Chuck Edwards]] (R-NC-11) connect over a federal earmark for a Real-Time Intelligence Center; accounts differ on who initiated the contact ([[Chuck Edwards|his page]] documents both versions). Edwards signs the formal request letter on **May 22, 2024**; his entire written justification is one sentence. Council is not informed. Residents learn of this sequence only later, through the work of [Communities Against Mass Surveillance](https://camsavl.org/rtic-funding-vote/).

**April 2024: Flock cameras go live.** APD deploys 11 Flock Safety license plate readers. Between April 2024 and December 2025 they scan roughly **3.1 million plates**, in a city of about 95,000 people, with data shared with agencies across a **500-mile, multi-state radius**. APD also runs Axon in-car plate readers and roughly two dozen drones. ([Asheville Watchdog](https://avlwatchdog.org/opinion-youre-on-camera-whether-you-like-it-or-not-but-asheville-needs-to-be-more-transparent-about-video-surveillance-proposal/), [Annie Dance / Cops & Congress](https://anniedance.substack.com/p/asheville-expands-regional-surveillance))

**March 7, 2025: Council hears about it, a year late, because Roney read the news.** Deputy Chief [[Jackie Stepp]] briefs council on the LPR program for the first time, admitting mobile readers had run for nearly a year, stationary readers "approximately two years," cross-jurisdiction sharing was live, and APD had access to **"over 177,000 cameras across the Eastern US"** through Flock and Axon partnerships, "and we've had that access." The briefing happened only because Kim Roney read about the cameras online and asked; the committee that would normally review it wasn't meeting. Full write-up with timestamps: [[2025-03-07 Agenda Briefing]].

**March 25, 2025: First RTIC money, buried on the consent agenda.** A $71,999 federal grant for RTIC equipment (video wall, workstations) is placed on the consent agenda, the part of the meeting reserved for routine, undebated items. Roney pulls it for discussion and submits **29 written questions** to APD about data access, control, and constitutional implications. The questions go unanswered. It passes 6–1, along with six other police funding items. Council member Sheneika Smith says on the record "I kind of feel robbed of having those conversations," and suggests a community forum; it is never held. Bo Hess carries the item with "we're not buying surveillance, we're buying chairs", while conceding the center "already exists." Full write-up: [[2025-03-25 City Council]]. ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/bpr-news/2025-03-26/last-night-at-council-asheville-approves-funding-for-police-surveillance-hub), [CAMS](https://camsavl.org/rtic-funding-vote/))

**February 2026: The $1.14M lands.** The Community Project Funding earmark secured by Rep. [[Chuck Edwards]] comes through: $1.14 million, administered by the Department of Justice. The DOJ's conditions are published only after acceptance. ([CAMS](https://camsavl.org/rtic-funding-vote/))

## The fight (April–May 2026)

**April 11, 2026: The education begins.** Three days before the council vote, SASSI (Southerners Against Surveillance Systems & Infrastructure) holds "You Are Being Watched: An Introduction to Police Surveillance in Buncombe County" at Firestorm Books. ([SASSI](https://sassisouth.org))

**April 14, 2026: Too many people show up; the vote disappears.** More than 40 residents arrive to speak against accepting the $1.14M. The item is pulled from the agenda right before the meeting. Jen Hampton of Asheville Food and Beverage United, at the podium anyway: "Let's call it what it actually is: a centralized hub for spying on the people of Asheville." Mayor Manheimer concedes the issue "warrants a full conversation." ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-04-15/asheville-city-council-punts-vote-on-police-tech-funding-and-suggests-property-tax-hike))

**April 27, 2026: Even the Watchdog objects.** Asheville Watchdog's John Boyle (no police abolitionist) publishes an opinion piece criticizing the city for burying the initiative on consent agendas and demanding transparency. The cost breakdown he reports: ~$400K video wall, $467K in software over 7–8 years, $250K operational. ([Asheville Watchdog](https://avlwatchdog.org/opinion-youre-on-camera-whether-you-like-it-or-not-but-asheville-needs-to-be-more-transparent-about-video-surveillance-proposal/))

**April 28, 2026: The "work session."** The Policy, Finance and Infrastructure Committee hears presentations from Axon and Flock sales representatives alongside Interim Chief Jackie Stepp. No public comment is allowed. CAMS members publish a [fact-check one-pager](https://www.sunshinelabs.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Asheville-RTIC-%E2%80%93-1-Pager.pdf) responding to vendor claims. ([CAMS](https://camsavl.org/rtic-funding-vote/))

**May 11, 2026: GAP draws the line.** The Government Accountability Project of Asheville publishes its position: reject the RTIC/Axon expansion until real oversight exists: no civilian oversight body, no facial recognition ban, vendor lock-in, and no final negotiated contract available for council to review. ([GAP](https://gapavl.org/asheville-should-reject-the-proposed-rtic-axon-surveillance-expansion-until-real-oversight-and-accountability-exist/))

**May 12, 2026: Passed at midnight.** The RTIC items are placed at the end of a budget meeting. Public comment is capped at one hour; speakers wait past 10:30 PM; **21 signed-up residents are sent home without speaking**. Of the ~36 who do speak, nearly all oppose. The council approves the $1.14M grant and a **7.5-year contract with Axon** (body cameras, tasers, drones, interview-room tech, and Fusus software) without the final contract text available for review. Roney, the only no, presents ACLU and EFF concerns. Residents chant "Shame!" as it passes. Council member Bo Hess attaches a resolution stating the tech will be used "solely for lawful purposes." ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-13/asheville-council-accepts-police-tech-funding-hears-budget-proposal), [828NewsNow](https://828newsnow.com/news/228822-asheville-oks-real-time-police-intelligence-center-despite-public-backlash/), [Enlace Latino NC](https://enlacelatinonc.org/en/Asheville-City-Council-approves-creation-of-real-time-intelligence-center/), [People's World](https://www.peoplesworld.org/article/surveillance-state-major-police-intelligence-center-coming-to-asheville-n-c/))

> [!note] Verified from the city's own video
> Press coverage disagreed on the vote count (4–1, 5–1, and 6–1 all appeared). The city's own recording settles it: the chair states on video, "the vote was **6 to 1** in favor of item B1," and B2 followed 6–1 as well. The B1 vote was taken **off-microphone amid shouting, seconds after public comment closed**; a council member had to announce "in case you didn't notice, there was just a vote." Full minute-by-minute reconstruction with video timestamps: [[2026-05-12 City Council]].

**May 25, 2026: RE:FUSUS.** CAMS throws a surveillance-awareness social at DayTrip: DJ sets and a Q&A with [[Benn Jordan]] (the researcher whose Flock investigations reached national press) plus Kim Roney, Colonel Panic, and Patrick Conant. ([Sunshine Labs](https://www.sunshinelabs.org/sos-sunshine-over-surveillance/))

**May 28, 2026: The mayoral race becomes the referendum.** At the first Roney–Manheimer debate, surveillance is a headline clash. Roney raises "a billionaire-owned, AI-fueled, for-profit tech company... working with ICE" surveilling without warrants; Manheimer frames the grant as cost savings. ([BPR](https://www.bpr.org/politics-government/2026-05-28/at-the-first-asheville-mayor-debate-roney-and-manheimer-clash-over-transparency-and-surveillance))

## Aftermath and accountability (June 2026– )

**June 4, 2026: The tours begin.** APD opens bi-weekly public tours of the RTIC space, framed as transparency "following public debates over privacy." Every other Thursday, 2–4 PM; register at 828-259-5881. Go, and take notes. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/asheville-police-department-tours-new-real-time-intelligence-center-approved-federal-funds-social-media-transparency-privacy-public-debates-analyze-data-emergencies-register))

**July 7, 2026: The state doubles down.** The NC budget makes the SBI's highway license plate reader program permanent, authorizing ALPRs on state-maintained roads. The SBI's stated goal: a camera at every entrance and exit to the state. ([WRAL](https://www.wral.com/news/nccapitol/surveillance-drivers-camera-alpr-license-plate-reader-nc-budget-public-safety-privacy-july-2026/), [Daily Tar Heel](https://dailytarheel.com/article/city-ai-highway-surveillance-north-carolina-budget-20260728))

**July 8, 2026: What "oversight" means so far.** A WLOS investigation details the RTIC's planned integrations (Flock ALPRs, body and car cameras, drones, voluntary private cameras in Fusus's "one pane of glass") and APD's internal controls: quarterly audits, keycard reviews, and search-term blocks. APD tech manager [[Jimmy Wingo|James Wingo]]: blocks cover "anything to do with immigration, anything to do with reproductive issues." All of it is self-administered. Operational target: **spring/summer 2027**. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/news-13-investigates/inside-asheville-police-department-apd-future-real-time-intelligence-center-cameras-license-plate-readers-car-body-cams-drones-technology-investigations))

**July 15, 2026: Macon County shows it can be undone.** County commissioners vote **5–0** to end their $47,000 Flock contract; 12 cameras come down. The push came largely from Republican residents and officials. See [[WNC and NC]]. ([Smoky Mountain News](https://smokymountainnews.com/news/news-latest/de-flocking-macon-county-votes-to-let-alpr-contract-lapse/))

**July 29 & August 5, 2026: NC officers charged for abusing Flock.** A Charlotte officer becomes the first in NC criminally charged for an unauthorized Flock search; a week later a Mooresville officer is arrested for using Flock to stalk his ex-wife: 31 searches. ([WNC/state coverage via NCLocal](https://nclocal.org/2026/07/30/balancing-public-safety-and-privacy-wnc-wrestles-with-surveillance-issues/))

**August 7, 2026: Carrier Park.** A Flock camera near Carrier Park is found vandalized. ([WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/gallery/asheville-flock-camera-vandalized-carrier-park-license-plate-reader-damaged))

**August 16–22, 2026: National Week of Action.** Asheville joins the DeFlock National Week of Action Against ALPRs: a West Asheville kickoff potluck, an online panel, and a Friday celebration at Cooperative Coffee. ([CAMS](https://camsavl.org), [DeFlock](https://deflock.org))
